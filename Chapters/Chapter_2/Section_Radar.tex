\section{One source for personal details}
Both entry points read \texttt{ThesisDetails.tex}. The names are fictional, the
year is \texttt{20XX}, the date is \texttt{XX/XX/20XX}, and the ISBN fields use
\texttt{XXX-XX-XXX-XXXX-X}. These examples are meant to look like book metadata
without pretending to be assigned identifiers. Replace both print and digital
ISBNs with the identifiers issued for your actual editions.

\begin{lstlisting}
\MyThesis[A Thesis You Can Make Your Own]
{A Guide to This Layout}
{Writing, Organising and Personalising a Doctoral Thesis}
{Alex Morgan}{A.~Morgan}{20XX}
\MyDefense[Monday]{XX}{Month}{XX:XX}
\end{lstlisting}
The optional argument of \verb|\MyThesis| is the first title line. Its five
mandatory arguments are the second title line, subtitle, full name, short name
and year. The optional argument of \verb|\MyDefense| is the weekday; its three
mandatory arguments are day, month and time. These setters create commands,
so call each only once. Edit their existing calls rather than adding another.

The setters create these commands:
\begin{lstlisting}
\thesistitleA      \thesistitleB      \thesissubtitle
\thesisauthor      \thesisauthorshort \thesisyear
\defenseweekday   \defenseday        \defensemonth
\defensetime
\end{lstlisting}
The combined
\verb|\thesistitle| is used on formal pages and in PDF metadata.
The separate \verb|\defensedate| is a short display date: keep it consistent
with the expanded date fields. Tildes between initials and surnames prevent
unhelpful line breaks. Escape special characters in metadata, for example
\verb|Research \& Design|.

The Dutch title and subtitle, supervisors, committee chair, printer and group
also have named fields in this file. Committee member rows remain in
\texttt{Title\_Preamb.tex}, where their number can be changed naturally.
